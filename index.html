About

I am a fourth-year Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Duke University, advised by Professor Jian Pei. I make LLMs and AI agents trustworthy for real-world deployment: reliable, low-cost evaluation, privacy, and generalization.

I am now pushing into emerging frontiers: agentic commerce (AI agents that shop for users), person distillation (capturing a real person’s expertise in AI), and LLMs that know when to ask questions.

Research paper in submission

Amazon Bedrock Guardrails · Deep Engine-Science · Seattle, WA

I developed Judge2Vec, which turns each LLM judge into a computable embedding of its competence across evaluation dimensions, without human labels, to assemble small, complementary juries that are both reliable and cheap.

Less is more: a jury of just 4 of 19 LLMs beat querying all 19 at 83.9% lower cost, achieving state-of-the-art results on 8 text and agentic benchmarks.

Judge2Vec also handles brand-new tasks with zero task data (retaining 98.5% of performance), adapts to any cost budget, and extends to multimodal inputs.

AT&T Chief Data Office

Summer 2025

Graduate Research Intern

Research paper in submission

Bedminster, NJ · Mentors: Divesh Srivastava and Yaron Kanza

I enabled LLMs to forget a user’s private data on request without retraining (GDPR/CCPA): the model learns only public data, private data sits in a separate deletable database (kNN-LM), and an adaptive selector picks which source answers.

I showed that deleting the data is not enough: the retrieval embeddings decide both answer quality and whether deleted facts leak. I designed embeddings that stay accurate yet reveal nothing once deleted.

The system scales to 19.6M entries (300 GB) with user-level deletion; 1,000× more data made answers only 4.9× slower.

Selected Research Projects

Agentic commerce

Ongoing

As AI agents begin to search and buy on users’ behalf, marketplaces see only the agents’ actions, never the users’ intent. I formulated how platforms should serve these agents, and I am building an LLM-based policy that predicts agent behavior and optimizes revenue across multi-turn interactions.

Person distillation

Preprint · 2026

I laid the foundations for distilling a real person into AI: turning their documents, messages, and decisions into a model of their expertise and judgment, not just their style, so AI agents and “digital coworkers” can carry their know-how forward.

  1. Source evidence
  2. Person representations
  3. Distillation methods
  4. Evaluation

The paper is the field’s first comprehensive framework. It organizes person distillation as this four-stage lifecycle, with a taxonomy for each stage, and maps six families of distillation methods: prompt-based profiles, memory systems, fine-tuned models and adapters, reward models of a person’s judgment, behavioral trajectories, and hybrid, revisable designs.

Its evaluation framework asks whether an AI captures the person rather than their role, using temporal hold-outs, comparisons against matched peers, and boundary probes that check whether the AI knows when the evidence runs out.

LLM-based agent generalizability

ACM Computing Surveys · 2026

Will an agent that works in one setting still work in the next? I defined LLM-based agent generalizability across instructions, tasks, environments, and domains, and organized evaluation and improvement methods by the backbone LLM, specialized components, and their interactions. The survey also distinguishes frameworks that can be adapted to different settings from agents that truly generalize beyond their fine-tuning data.

The work grew into two tutorials I co-organized, at KDD 2026 and PAKDD 2026.

MPCEval: Multi-party conversation evaluation

KDD 2026 · Oral presentation

I built a reproducible benchmark for AI-generated group conversations, such as meetings and team chats. It scores who speaks next, what they say, and whether the two fit, at both the turn and conversation level, with no reference answers needed. We evaluated diverse generation methods on collaborative problem-solving, scripted dialogue, and real enterprise discussions.

ACM SIGKDD 2026, Datasets and Benchmarks Track

Privacy attacks and defenses

I showed how private data can leak through knowledge distillation (membership-inference attacks on teacher–student models) and across sequential data releases (a learned attack using hidden Markov models and reinforcement learning), and proposed mitigations. I also designed defenses that protect data buyers’ privacy in data markets.
